# Quick Product Validation: Test Ideas in Days

You don't need a polished product to learn whether people care. Quick validation is about designing small experiments that give you real evidence — fast.

## 1. Pick one riskiest assumption
Every idea has one thing that determines if it can work (e.g., "Will people pay $5/month for this?"). Write that assumption down and make it the focus of your test.

## 2. Design the simplest experiment
Examples:
- A one-page landing with an email signup and a pricing option to test interest.
- A short survey or 5-minute interview for qualitative feedback.
- A paid ad driving to a signup page to measure click-through and conversion.

Keep it cheap and fast. The goal is signal, not polish.

## 3. Build just enough
Use no-code tools, a single static page, or manual processes (a concierge MVP) to simulate the experience. Don't spend more than a day or two for a first round.

## 4. Talk to users
Run 10–20 quick interviews. Watch people try the concept and listen to the language they use. Actual user quotes are more valuable than internal opinions.

## 5. Measure the right thing
Pick 1–2 metrics tied to your assumption (e.g., signup rate, paid conversion, booking intent). Measure consistently and act on the result.

## 6. Iterate or kill
If the experiment shows promise, make the next step slightly bigger: add a simple flow, test pricing variations, or prototype the core feature. If it fails, log lessons and move on quickly.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Single riskiest assumption defined
- [ ] One clear experiment with success criteria
- [ ] Ready-to-launch prototype (even if manual)
- [ ] 10–20 user conversations scheduled
- [ ] Decision made within a week

## Final thought
Validation is about exposure to reality. Spend less time building and more time learning. Fast, cheap experiments help you avoid long, expensive wrong turns — and get you to the right product, faster.
